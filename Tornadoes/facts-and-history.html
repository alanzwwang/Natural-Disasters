🌪️ Tornadoes: Facts & History

Tornadoes — Slide 1 of 3: Facts & History

1. Facts & History 2. Pictures 3. Famous Disasters

A tornado is a violently spinning column of air that stretches down from a thunderstorm cloud to the ground. Inside a big thunderstorm, winds blowing at different speeds and heights can make the air start rolling like a tube, and then the storm tips that tube upright into a spinning funnel. When the funnel touches the ground — it's a tornado!

Tornadoes have the fastest winds on Earth — the strongest can spin at nearly 500 kilometres per hour! Scientists rate them on the Enhanced Fujita scale, from EF0 (light damage) to EF5 (incredible damage), mostly by looking at the destruction they leave behind.

🗺️ Where do tornadoes happen?

Tornadoes can happen almost anywhere, but the champion spot is Tornado Alley in the middle of the USA. There, warm damp air from the Gulf of Mexico crashes into cold dry air from Canada and dry desert air from the west — perfect tornado ingredients! The USA gets over 1,000 tornadoes every year.

📜 A bit of history

Before weather radar, people had almost no warning when a tornado was coming. In 1948, two Air Force officers noticed that storms on the new radar screens came with hook shapes — and made the first successful tornado forecast in history, saving an air base's planes just in time!

⚡ Amazing fact! Tornadoes have picked up some very strange things: fish, frogs, and even jelly have rained from the sky after tornadoes sucked them out of ponds! A tornado once carried a cancelled cheque 360 kilometres through the air to another state.
