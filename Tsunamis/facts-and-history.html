🌊 Tsunamis: Facts & History

Tsunamis — Slide 1 of 3: Facts & History

1. Facts & History 2. Pictures 3. Famous Disasters

A tsunami is a series of giant ocean waves caused by something suddenly moving a huge amount of sea water — usually an underwater earthquake, but sometimes an undersea volcano or landslide. The word "tsunami" comes from Japanese and means "harbour wave".

Out in the deep ocean, a tsunami might be only a metre tall but it travels incredibly fast — up to 800 kilometres per hour, as fast as a jet plane! When it reaches shallow water near the coast, it slows down and the water piles up into enormous waves that can be taller than a house.

⚠️ Nature's warning signs

Before a tsunami, the sea sometimes pulls back far from the shore, leaving the sea floor bare — this is a huge warning to run to high ground! Animals often sense danger too; in 2004, elephants in Thailand ran uphill before the waves arrived.

📜 A bit of history

After a terrible tsunami hit Hawaii in 1946, countries around the Pacific Ocean worked together to build a tsunami warning system. Today, special buoys floating in the ocean can feel a tsunami passing underneath and radio a warning to shore, saving many lives.

⚡ Amazing fact! A tsunami is not just one wave — it is a whole train of waves that can keep arriving for hours! And the first wave is often NOT the biggest one, which is why rescuers tell people to stay on high ground until officials say it is safe.
