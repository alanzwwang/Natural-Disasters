🌀 Hurricanes: Facts & History

Hurricanes — Slide 1 of 3: Facts & History

1. Facts & History 2. Pictures 3. Famous Disasters

A hurricane is a gigantic spinning storm that forms over warm tropical ocean water. Warm water is its fuel: the sun heats the sea, water evaporates and rises, and the storm starts to spin because of Earth's rotation. In the middle is a calm, clear hole called the eye, surrounded by the eyewall — where the wildest winds of all are found.

The same storm has different names in different places: in the Atlantic it's a hurricane, in the western Pacific it's a typhoon, and in the Indian Ocean it's a cyclone. They are all the same kind of storm! Scientists rate their strength from Category 1 (weakest) to Category 5 (strongest).

💨 How fast is the wind?

To be called a hurricane, a storm needs winds of at least 119 kilometres per hour — faster than cars drive on the motorway! The strongest hurricanes can have winds over 250 kilometres per hour, fast enough to rip roofs off houses and snap trees.

📜 A bit of history

Hurricanes get people's names so that weather forecasters can talk about them easily. Before names, they were tracked by numbers, which got confusing when two storms spun at once! If a hurricane is especially terrible, its name is retired forever — like Katrina in 2005.

⚡ Amazing fact! A big hurricane can release as much energy in one day as 10,000 nuclear bombs! Luckily, satellites watch every hurricane from space, so we usually get days of warning before one arrives. No hurricane has ever sneaked up on us since the satellite age began.
