Magnitude, Seismic Moment and Energy

Lesson 5 of 10

Key idea of this section

Amplitude magnitudes

Richter (1935) defined local magnitude for southern California as the base-10 logarithm of the maximum trace amplitude, in millimetres, on a standard Wood–Anderson seismograph, corrected to a reference distance. An event that produces 1 mm at 100 km has ML = 3. A modern calibration of the distance correction (Hutton and Boore, 1987) is

ML = log₁₀ A + 1.11 log₁₀(r/100) + 0.00189 (r − 100) + 3.0     (A in mm, r in km)

Gutenberg extended the concept to teleseismic distances. The body-wave magnitude mb uses the amplitude-to-period ratio of P waves at about 1 s period. The surface-wave magnitude Ms uses Rayleigh waves at about 20 s period, and in the IASPEI "Prague" form (Vaněk et al., 1962) is

Ms = log₁₀(A/T) + 1.66 log₁₀ Δ + 3.3     (A in μm, Δ in degrees, 20° ≤ Δ ≤ 160°)

All amplitude magnitudes saturate. Once the rupture is much longer than the wavelength being measured, the amplitude at that period stops growing. mb saturates near 6–6.5, Ms near 8–8.5 and ML near 6.5–7. The 1960 Chile earthquake had Ms ≈ 8.5 but released roughly two to four times more moment than any event recorded since.

Seismic moment and moment magnitude

Aki (1966) showed that the low-frequency level of the far-field displacement spectrum is proportional to the seismic moment M₀ = μAD̄, where μ is the rigidity (about 3 × 10¹⁰ Pa in the crust and 4–7 × 10¹⁰ Pa on deeper megathrusts), A is the rupture area and D̄ is the average slip. Moment is a physical measure of the size of the source that does not saturate. Kanamori (1977) and Hanks and Kanamori (1979) defined moment magnitude so as to match Ms and ML in their unsaturated ranges. In the IASPEI standard form, with M₀ in N·m:

Mw = (2/3)(log₁₀ M₀ − 9.1)

One unit of Mw corresponds to a factor of 101.5 ≈ 31.6 in moment, and two units to a factor of 1,000. Representative values are given below.

Event                          M₀ (N·m)          Mw
Chile 1960                     ≈ 2 × 10²³        9.4–9.6
Tōhoku 2011                    ≈ 4–5 × 10²²      9.0–9.1
San Francisco 1906             ≈ 6–9 × 10²⁰      7.8–7.9
Northridge 1994                ≈ 1.2 × 10¹⁹      6.7

Five great earthquakes, Chile 1960, Alaska 1964, Sumatra 2004, Tōhoku 2011 and Kamchatka 1952, account for roughly half of all seismic moment released since 1900.

The source spectrum, corner frequency and stress drop

In the ω² model (Aki, 1967; Brune, 1970), the far-field displacement spectrum is flat at a level proportional to M₀ below a corner frequency fc and falls as f−2 above it. Brune related fc to the radius of an equivalent circular source:

r = 2.34 VS / (2π fc) ;    Δσ = 7 M₀ / (16 r³)

Because fc enters as a cube, stress-drop estimates are uncertain by a factor of several. With constant stress drop, fc scales as M₀−1/3, so large earthquakes are relatively rich in long-period energy. This matters for tall buildings, long bridges and liquid sloshing in storage tanks.

Radiated energy

Gutenberg and Richter (1956) gave log₁₀ E (J) = 1.5 Ms + 4.8. The radiated energy Es relates to moment through the scaled energy Es/M₀, which is typically about 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴. Kanamori (1977) used Es ≈ M₀/(2 × 10⁴) = 5 × 10⁻⁵ M₀. The energy magnitude Me, computed directly from broadband radiated energy, can differ from Mw by several tenths of a unit and is a better guide to damage potential at high frequencies. Radiated energy is only a fraction of the total work done during faulting. Most of the work goes into frictional heat and fracture energy, and the partitioning is a central research question.

Exercise

  • (a)
    A Wood–Anderson instrument at r = 100 km records a maximum amplitude of 10 mm. What is ML?
  • (b)
    A megathrust rupture is 400 km long and 200 km wide, with average slip 12 m and μ = 40 GPa. Compute M₀ and Mw.
  • (c)
    By what factor does the seismic moment of an Mw 9.1 earthquake exceed that of an Mw 7.8?
  • (d)
    An earthquake has M₀ = 1 × 10¹⁷ N·m and a corner frequency of 0.5 Hz, with VS = 3.5 km/s. Find Mw, the Brune source radius and the stress drop.
Show solution

Answers: (a) ML = log₁₀ 10 + 0 + 0 + 3.0 = 4.0. (b) M₀ = 4 × 10¹⁰ × (4 × 10⁵ × 2 × 10⁵) × 12 = 3.84 × 10²² N·m; Mw = (2/3)(22.58 − 9.1) ≈ 9.0, comparable to Tōhoku. (c) 101.5 × 1.3 = 101.95 ≈ 89. Radiated energy scales by about the same factor, assuming similar scaled energy. (d) Mw = (2/3)(17 − 9.1) ≈ 5.3. r = 2.34 × 3,500 / (2π × 0.5) ≈ 2,610 m. Δσ = 7 × 10¹⁷ / (16 × 2,607³) ≈ 2.5 MPa, a typical value.
