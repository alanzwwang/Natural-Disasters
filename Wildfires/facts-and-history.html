🔥 Wildfires: Facts & History

Wildfires — Slide 1 of 3: Facts & History

1. Facts & History 2. Pictures 3. Famous Disasters

A wildfire is a large fire that spreads out of control through forests, grasslands, or bush. Fires need three things, called the fire triangle: fuel (dry grass, leaves, and trees), heat (a spark or flame), and oxygen (from the air). Take any one of these away and the fire dies — that's how firefighters beat them!

Wildfires can start from lightning strikes, but most are started by people — campfires not put out properly, dropped glass, or sparks from machines. Hot, dry, windy weather makes fires spread incredibly fast: a grass fire can move faster than a person can run!

🌱 Wait — fire can be GOOD?

Surprise! Some forests actually need fire. Heat makes the cones of some pine trees pop open and drop their seeds, and fire clears away old plants so sunlight can reach new ones. For thousands of years, Indigenous peoples around the world used small, careful "good fires" to keep forests healthy and stop big fires from building up.

📜 A bit of history

One famous firefighter isn't human at all — it's Smokey Bear! Created in 1944 in the USA, Smokey taught generations of kids that "only YOU can prevent wildfires." The idea came after a real bear cub was rescued from a wildfire in 1950 and became a national symbol.

⚡ Amazing fact! Wildfires can move uphill twice as fast as on flat ground, because the flames heat the grass and trees above them, drying them out before the fire even arrives. Some eucalyptus trees in Australia actually help fires along — their leaves are full of flammable oil!
