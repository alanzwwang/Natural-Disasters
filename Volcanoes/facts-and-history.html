🌋 Volcanic Eruptions: Facts & History

Volcanic Eruptions — Slide 1 of 3: Facts & History

1. Facts & History 2. Pictures 3. Famous Disasters

A volcanic eruption happens when melted rock, hot gases, and ash burst out of an opening in Earth's crust. Deep underground it is so hot that rock melts into a thick glowing liquid called magma. Magma is lighter than the rock around it, so it rises and collects in a pocket called a magma chamber.

When the pressure gets too big — POP! — the volcano erupts! Once magma reaches the surface it gets a new name: lava. Eruptions can ooze slowly like syrup or explode like a giant bottle of fizzy drink that has been shaken up.

🗺️ Where do eruptions happen?

Most volcanoes sit along the edges of giant puzzle pieces of Earth's crust called tectonic plates. Many of them form a huge circle around the Pacific Ocean called the Ring of Fire — about 75% of the world's volcanoes live there!

📜 A bit of history

People have watched volcanoes for thousands of years. The ancient Romans believed the god of fire, Vulcan, worked his forge under a volcano — and that is where the word "volcano" comes from! The word "lava" comes from an Italian word meaning "to slide or fall."

⚡ Amazing fact! There are more than 1,500 active volcanoes on Earth right now — and that doesn't even count the ones hiding under the ocean! The biggest volcano in our solar system is Olympus Mons on Mars, which is about two and a half times taller than Mount Everest.
